About BharatWatch

BharatWatch is a public-data knowledge graph that links Indian politicians and their declared assets and family ties to companies, government contracts, fund flows and public spending — and automatically surfaces statistical red-flag patterns for journalists, researchers and citizens to investigate further. It is inspired by Bruno César's Brazilian public-spending graph, adapted to Indian identifiers and data sources.

Methodology: public data only

Every record is ingested from official, publicly accessible portals. No private scraping, no restricted-access sources:

SourceWhat we useLinking key
ECI Affidavit Portal / MyNeta (ADR)Candidate assets, liabilities, income, criminal cases, family detailsPAN, name + constituency
MCA Master Data / data.gov.inCompany registrations, directors, appointments, chargesCIN, DIN, PAN
CPPP / eProcureTenders, bids, contract awardsCIN, supplier name
GeMProcurement orders, buyer/seller detailsGSTIN, CIN
PFMSScheme expenditures, payments, DBT dashboardsScheme + entity name
CVC public lists, GST search, state portalsCross-checks and supplementsGSTIN, PAN

Records are merged with deterministic keys first (PAN > DIN > CIN), then AI-assisted fuzzy name matching (honorifics, transliteration, Pvt/Ltd variants). Uncertain matches go to a human review queue — they are never auto-merged.

Detection patterns

  • Family contracts — politician → family member (affidavit) → director (DIN) → company → government contract.
  • Incompatible asset growth — affidavit-to-affidavit growth beyond a generous savings + 12%/yr appreciation ceiling.
  • Repeated awards — a connected supplier winning a dominant share of contracts from a body the politician oversees.
  • Ghost entities — companies winning public orders within ~120 days of incorporation, especially at shared addresses.
  • Fund loops — money cycling company → trust → company within one family network.

Risk scores (0–100%) reflect pattern strength and value involved. They are statistical indicators, not findings of guilt.

Limitations & ethics

  • Public records contain errors, lags and gaps; identity resolution can mis-match similar names.
  • A flag is a starting point for human investigation — innocent explanations are common (e.g. a competitive bid legitimately won by a relative's firm).
  • Read-only public access; data ingestion is admin-controlled and source-attributed.
  • Corrections: any affected person may request review of source records via the contact channel of the deployment operator.
